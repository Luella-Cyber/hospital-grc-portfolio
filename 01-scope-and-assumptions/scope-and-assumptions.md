# Scope and Assumptions

## Purpose

This case study assesses information security and physical security risk for a hypothetical state-run psychiatric hospital. It is a portfolio exercise built from public information only. It is not affiliated with any real hospital or agency, and it does not describe any real facility, procedure, system, or incident.

## The hypothetical organization

A state-run inpatient psychiatric hospital with:

- Several inpatient units and a campus of buildings
- Clinical staff, administrative staff, security officers, and contractors
- An electronic health record (EHR) system holding patient information
- Visitors, including family members and approved special visits
- Third-party vendors that maintain systems and equipment

## In scope

- Physical access to buildings and patient areas, including doors, keys, and badges
- Visitor management and screening
- Protection of electronic protected health information (ePHI) in the EHR and related systems
- Staff and contractor access to records and systems
- Incident reporting and escalation
- Third-party and vendor access
- Security awareness for staff

## Out of scope

- Clinical care and treatment decisions
- Any real facility procedures, schedules, staffing levels, or floor plans
- Any real incident, patient, or employee information
- Medical device safety
- Legal conclusions about compliance

## Frameworks and method

- **HIPAA Security Rule:** administrative, physical, and technical safeguards for ePHI
- **NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0:** used for the control mapping
- **Risk rating:** likelihood and impact on a 1 to 5 scale, following the approach in NIST SP 800-30

## Assumptions

- The hospital is a composite. Its systems and controls are typical of the industry and not specific to any one site.
- Doors are secured with a mix of keys and electronic badges.
- Visitors check in at a front desk and are screened before entering patient areas.
- The EHR is a commercial product maintained by a vendor with remote access.
- Staff work in shifts and rely on written handoffs between teams.
- Paper records exist but are limited.
- Security officers are on site at all times.

## Limitations

This is an educational exercise. Findings are illustrative, are not an audit of any real organization, and are not legal advice.
